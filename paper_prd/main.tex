\begin{abstract}
We present a compact, submission-facing version of the Projection Spectral Layer Theory (PSLT) as a conditional EFT-level spectral-layer occupancy closure on a fixed projected two-center conformal background.
The central object is a normalized layer probability
\(P_N=W_N/\sum_K W_K\), where \(W_N\) combines an effective phase-space weight \(g_N\), a rank-2 kinetic rate \(\Gamma_N\), and a visibility factor \(B_N\).
The claim of this short manuscript is deliberately calibrated: under the stated assumptions and finite audit domains, the release baseline shows first-three-layer occupancy concentration and a finite-domain no-fourth-bound-layer kinetic certificate.
It is not presented as a complete EYMH derivation of exactly three Standard Model fermion generations, nor as an absolute Higgs-sector prediction.
The long technical manuscript is treated as a supplementary audit companion containing the proposition chains, certificates, artifact registry, and negative/diagnostic branches.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Claim Calibration}
\label{sec:intro}

The Standard Model accommodates three fermion families but does not determine the family count dynamically.
Traditional flavor, GUT, and compactification approaches organize charges or correlations among Yukawa couplings, while the family multiplicity is often supplied as an external input or model-building choice~\cite{Cabibbo1963,KobayashiMaskawa1973,MakiNakagawaSakata1962,FroggattNielsen1979,AltarelliFeruglio2010,Feruglio2015,CandelasHorowitzStromingerWitten1985,GeorgiGlashow1974,FritzschMinkowski1975,PatiSalam1974,GurseyRamondSikivie1976,Slansky1981}.
PSLT asks a narrower question: whether a projected geometric spectral problem, combined with a specified EFT closure, yields a reproducible concentration of observable weight in the first three spectral layers.

The reason for this narrower formulation is not merely rhetorical.
In a conventional family-count theorem one would first identify a microscopic Hilbert space of chiral Standard Model fermions and then prove that its protected low-energy multiplicity is exactly three.
The current PSLT release does not yet provide that theorem.
Instead, it supplies a computable chain
\begin{equation}
    \Omega(\rho,z;\Dsep)
    \longrightarrow
    V_{\rm eff}
    \longrightarrow
    \{\omega_N,S_N\}
    \longrightarrow
    \Gamma_N
    \longrightarrow
    P_N,
    \label{eq:chain_intro}
\end{equation}
and asks whether this chain is internally stable, reproducible, and physically interpretable under explicitly stated assumptions.
The distinction matters for review: the evidence presented below supports the conditional closure in Eq.~\eqref{eq:chain_intro}; it should not be read as a hidden proof of an ultraviolet completion.

\subsection{Relation to familiar flavor and unification programs}

Gauge-unification frameworks such as \(SU(5)\), \(SO(10)\), Pati--Salam, \(E_6\), and related group-theoretic embeddings organize one Standard Model family into larger representations and constrain charge assignments.
Flavor-symmetry models organize observed mixing patterns and Yukawa hierarchies.
String or geometric compactifications can produce family multiplicities through topological data, but the multiplicity is then tied to the chosen compactification and bundle sector.
The present work is deliberately orthogonal to those goals.
It does not propose a new charge-unification group and does not replace CKM/PMNS flavor phenomenology.
It proposes a projected spectral-layer occupancy model whose output is a normalized scan weight.

This positioning is important because the same integer ``three'' can mean three different things:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item three unpaired chiral Standard Model families in a microscopic fermion theory;
    \item three bound or low-lying layers of a projected scalar spectral operator;
    \item first-three-layer dominance of a normalized EFT occupancy measure.
\end{enumerate}
Only the third item is the baseline claim of this short manuscript.
The first item is reserved for a future protected-index completion, and the second item is not generally true at every audited value of \(\Dsep\): the finite-domain kinetic proxy certifies absence of a fourth bound layer, not the existence of exactly three bound modes everywhere.

This short PRD manuscript is designed to be read independently of the long technical companion.
Its physics claims are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A fixed projected two-center conformal background defines a computable scalar spectral operator and WKB barrier data.
    \item A rank-2 two-center kinetic closure maps localized spectral information into \(\Gamma_N(\Dsep,\eta)\).
    \item A bounded effective phase-space weight \(g_N\), kinetic rate \(\Gamma_N\), and visibility factor \(B_N\) define the scan-level occupancy probability \(P_N\).
    \item On the audited release grid, the first-three-layer occupancy ratio \(\Rthree=P_1+P_2+P_3\) is concentrated over most of the sampled domain, and the audited finite-volume kinetic proxy has no fourth bound layer.
    \item The \(H\to\mu\mu\) map is a reference-normalized diagnostic target-region comparison to ATLAS data~\cite{ATLAS_Hmumu}, not a formal global likelihood, exclusion, or absolute Higgs-sector prediction.
\end{enumerate}

The manuscript does \emph{not} claim a full action-level theorem proving exactly three Standard Model generations.
Optional protected \(\mathrm{Spin}^c\), DSS/Floquet, open-system, and static-width packages are treated as supplementary or diagnostic material unless explicitly promoted in a future revision.

\subsection{What would falsify or weaken the present closure}

Because the present article is a conditional computational closure, the relevant failure modes are also concrete.
The release would be weakened if the live figure-generation path failed to reproduce the registered \(\Rthree\) fractions, if the WKB table failed the relation \(r_1=\exp(-2S_1)\), if the high-\(N\) kinetic audit supported a fourth bound layer on the certified domain, or if the reference-normalized Higgs diagnostic were described as an absolute prediction.
Conversely, none of the following would by itself invalidate the present short claim: failure of the optional DSS/Floquet parent attempt, non-promotion of global static-width poles, or the absence of a complete protected \(\mathrm{Spin}^c\) family-count theorem.
Those are future strengthening routes, not assumptions silently used in the release numbers.

\section{Projected Two-Center Spectral Operator}
\label{sec:operator}

The geometric input is a static conformally flat background,
\begin{equation}
    g_{\mu\nu}(x)=\Omega^2(x)\eta_{\mu\nu},
\end{equation}
with two projected centers separated by \(\Dsep\).
For a Plummer regulator \(\epsilon\), the working conformal factor is
\begin{equation}
    \Omega(\rho,z;\Dsep)=1+a\left(\frac{1}{r_+}+\frac{1}{r_-}\right),\qquad
    r_\pm=\sqrt{\rho^2+(z\mp \Dsep/2)^2+\epsilon^2}.
    \label{eq:omega_twocenter}
\end{equation}
It solves the projected Poisson equation with two smeared unit sources.
Indeed, for
\begin{equation}
    f_\epsilon(r)=(r^2+\epsilon^2)^{-1/2},
\end{equation}
the three-dimensional radial Laplacian gives
\begin{equation}
    \nabla^2 f_\epsilon
    =f_\epsilon''+\frac{2}{r}f_\epsilon'
    =-\frac{3\epsilon^2}{(r^2+\epsilon^2)^{5/2}}.
    \label{eq:plummer_laplacian}
\end{equation}
Thus, if
\begin{equation}
    \rho_\epsilon(r)=\frac{3\epsilon^2}{4\pi(r^2+\epsilon^2)^{5/2}},
    \qquad
    \int_{\mathbb R^3}\rho_\epsilon(r)\,\dd^3x=1,
\end{equation}
then Eq.~\eqref{eq:omega_twocenter} obeys
\begin{equation}
    \nabla^2\Omega
    =
    -4\pi a\left[
        \rho_\epsilon(x-x_+)+\rho_\epsilon(x-x_-)
    \right].
    \label{eq:projected_poisson}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Conformal Klein--Gordon reduction}

For a scalar test field with non-minimal curvature coupling~\cite{BirrellDavies1982}, the conformal reduction gives
\begin{equation}
    \left[-\nabla^2+V_{\rm eff}(x)\right]\psi_N=\omega_N^2\psi_N,
    \label{eq:spectral_operator}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
    V_{\rm eff}=m_0^2\Omega^2+(1-6\xi)\Omega^{-1}\nabla^2\Omega.
    \label{eq:veff}
\end{equation}
For completeness, we give the derivation because it is one of the few analytic steps in the release chain that should be easy to audit.
Start from
\begin{equation}
    (\Box_g-m_0^2-\xi R)\Phi=0,\qquad
    \Phi(t,x)=\ee^{-i\omega t}\Omega^{-1}(x)\psi(x).
\end{equation}
In four dimensions,
\begin{equation}
    \left(\Box_g-\frac{1}{6}R\right)\Omega^{-1}\psi
    =
    \Omega^{-3}\Box_\eta\psi.
    \label{eq:conformal_identity}
\end{equation}
Substitution gives
\begin{equation}
    \Box_\eta\psi-m_0^2\Omega^2\psi+\left(\frac{1}{6}-\xi\right)\Omega^2R\psi=0.
\end{equation}
With a static \(\Omega\) and \(g_{\mu\nu}=\Omega^2\eta_{\mu\nu}\),
\begin{equation}
    R=-6\Omega^{-3}\nabla^2\Omega,
\end{equation}
and Eq.~\eqref{eq:veff} follows after separating the time dependence.
This derivation is local and kinematic.
It does not claim that Eq.~\eqref{eq:projected_poisson} has already been obtained as the unique solution of a complete backreacted EYMH system.

\subsection{Finite-volume operator used in the audits}

The numerical release works with finite-volume discretizations of Eq.~\eqref{eq:spectral_operator}.
Abstractly, let \(\mathcal D_L\) be the audited cylindrical or one-dimensional projected domain and impose the boundary condition used by the relevant certificate.
The finite-volume operator is
\begin{equation}
    H_{\Dsep}^{(L,h)}
    =
    -\Delta_h+V_{\rm eff}^{(h)}(\Dsep),
    \label{eq:finite_volume_operator}
\end{equation}
with \(h\) denoting the mesh scale.
Self-adjointness at the matrix level is not optional: it is what allows the spectral ordering, Sturm/min--max counting, and Riesz-projector transport statements later in the paper to be meaningful.
The short manuscript therefore reports only certified statements that can be traced back to a specified finite-volume operator chain.

\section{Rank-2 Occupancy Closure}
\label{sec:closure}

The scan observable is the normalized layer probability
\begin{equation}
    P_N(t_{\rm coh};\Dsep,\eta)=\frac{W_N(t_{\rm coh};\Dsep,\eta)}{\sum_K W_K(t_{\rm coh};\Dsep,\eta)},
    \qquad
    W_N=B_N g_N\left(1-\ee^{-\Gamma_N(\Dsep,\eta)t_{\rm coh}}\right).
    \label{eq:master}
\end{equation}
The three factors have distinct roles: \(g_N\) is an effective phase-space weight, \(\Gamma_N\) is a kinetic formation rate, and \(B_N\) is a visibility weight.
Figure~\ref{fig:master_closure} summarizes the short-paper closure chain and the associated claim firewall.

The normalization in Eq.~\eqref{eq:master} is conditional on the chosen scan window and on the set of layers retained in the denominator.
It is therefore a relative occupancy measure, not a conserved probability derived from a microscopic unitary time evolution.
For small \(\Gamma_Nt_{\rm coh}\), the kinetic factor behaves as
\begin{equation}
    1-\ee^{-\Gamma_Nt_{\rm coh}}
    =
    \Gamma_Nt_{\rm coh}+\Order((\Gamma_Nt_{\rm coh})^2),
\end{equation}
so the early-time ordering is controlled by \(B_Ng_N\Gamma_N\).
For large \(\Gamma_Nt_{\rm coh}\), the same factor saturates and the ordering is controlled by \(B_Ng_N\).
This simple interpolation is the reason \(t_{\rm coh}\) is treated as a closure parameter rather than as a directly measured lifetime.

For the kinetic module, the two localized lobes define an effective rank-2 subspace.
A representative closure matrix is
\begin{equation}
    M_N=r_N
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \Gamma_{N,a} & \chi\sqrt{\Gamma_{N,a}\Gamma_{N,b}}\\
        \chi\sqrt{\Gamma_{N,a}\Gamma_{N,b}} & \Gamma_{N,b}
    \end{pmatrix},
    \qquad r_N=\eta\ee^{-2S_N}.
    \label{eq:rank2_matrix}
\end{equation}
The formation rate is the positive part of the largest eigenvalue,
\begin{equation}
    \Gamma_N=\max\{0,\lambda_+(M_N)\}.
\end{equation}
Explicitly,
\begin{equation}
    \lambda_+(M_N)
    =
    r_N\left[
    \frac{\Gamma_{N,a}+\Gamma_{N,b}}{2}
    +
    \sqrt{
        \left(\frac{\Gamma_{N,a}-\Gamma_{N,b}}{2}\right)^2
        +
        \chi^2\Gamma_{N,a}\Gamma_{N,b}
    }
    \right].
    \label{eq:rank2_lamplus}
\end{equation}
Thus \(M_N\) is positive semidefinite whenever \(r_N\ge0\), \(\Gamma_{N,a,b}\ge0\), and \(|\chi|\le1\).
When \(\eta>1\), the factor \(r_N=\eta\ee^{-2S_N}\) should not be called a literal single-particle tunneling probability; it is a coarse-grained rate multiplier built from a WKB suppression and an effective amplitude scale.
The WKB action entering \(r_N\) is
\begin{equation}
    S_N=\int_{\mathcal B_N}\sqrt{(V_{\rm eff}-\omega_N^2)_+}\,\dd s.
    \label{eq:wkb_action}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Two-center reduction and Feshbach interpretation}

The rank-2 form in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rank2_matrix} can be read as a controlled ansatz for a two-lobe collective subspace.
Let \(P_N^{(2)}\) project onto the two localized lobe states \(|N,+\rangle\) and \(|N,-\rangle\), and let \(Q_N^{(2)}=1-P_N^{(2)}\).
If the eliminated complement has a stable gap, a Feshbach--Schur reduction~\cite{Feshbach1958} gives an effective operator
\begin{equation}
    K_N^{\rm eff}(E)
    =
    P_N^{(2)}K_NP_N^{(2)}
    -
    P_N^{(2)}K_NQ_N^{(2)}
    (Q_N^{(2)}K_NQ_N^{(2)}-E)^{-1}
    Q_N^{(2)}K_NP_N^{(2)}.
    \label{eq:feshbach_rank2}
\end{equation}
The release rank-2 matrix is the finite-dimensional closure of this structure after the diagonal lobe rates and off-diagonal coherent mixing are compressed to \(\Gamma_{N,a}\), \(\Gamma_{N,b}\), and \(\chi\).
The short paper does not require a full microscopic derivation of \(K_N\), but it does require the rank-2 compression to be stated as an assumption rather than smuggled in as a theorem.

\subsection{Effective phase-space and visibility weights}

The release \(g_N\) is best read as an effective shell-volume weight.
Legacy Cardy-like or holographic motivations remain useful background, but the baseline number entering Eq.~\eqref{eq:master} is the bounded two-dimensional phase-space profile audited in the long companion.
Likewise, \(B_N\) is a visibility factor: it controls how strongly an occupied layer appears in the chosen visible-sector diagnostic.
The neutral choice \(B_{N>3}=1\) is important because it prevents the absence of a fourth visible layer from being engineered by a visibility cutoff.
In the release interpretation, high-\(N\) control comes from the phase-space/tail prescription and the independent finite-domain kinetic certificate, not from hiding extra layers in \(B_N\).

\section{Projector Coherence Along \texorpdfstring{\(\Dsep\)}{Dsep}}
\label{sec:projector_transport}

A useful optional strengthening is the spectral-projector transport theorem along \(\Dsep\).
The point is modest but important: the low-mode labels \(N=1,2,3\) should be treated as transported projectors of a parameter-dependent operator family, not as unrelated pointwise eigenvectors.

Let \(H(\Dsep)\) be the finite-volume self-adjoint operator associated with Eq.~\eqref{eq:spectral_operator}, after the coordinate pullback to a fixed reference domain.
If a Riesz contour \(\Gamma_N(\Dsep)\) separates the \(N\)-th eigenvalue from the rest of the spectrum, then
\begin{equation}
    P_N(\Dsep)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma_N(\Dsep)}(z-H(\Dsep))^{-1}\,\dd z
    \label{eq:riesz_projector}
\end{equation}
varies continuously, and differentiably when \(H'(\Dsep)\) exists in operator norm~\cite{Kato1995}.
Differentiating the resolvent identity gives the standard expression
\begin{equation}
    P_N'(\Dsep)
    =
    \frac{1}{2\pi i}
    \oint_{\Gamma_N(\Dsep)}
    (z-H)^{-1}H'(\Dsep)(z-H)^{-1}
    \,\dd z,
    \label{eq:projector_derivative}
\end{equation}
where \(H=H(\Dsep)\) inside the integrand.
If the contour has length \(|\Gamma_N|\) and distance at least \(\delta_N\) from the rest of the spectrum, then the elementary bound
\begin{equation}
    \|P_N'(\Dsep)\|
    \le
    \frac{|\Gamma_N|}{2\pi}
    \frac{\|H'(\Dsep)\|}{\delta_N^2}
    \label{eq:projector_derivative_bound}
\end{equation}
follows immediately.
This bound is analytically clean but often numerically pessimistic because \(\|H'\|\) is dominated by the regulated Plummer core rather than by the low-mode matrix elements.

The T-series audit therefore separates three levels.
T2 gives an explicit analytic envelope for \(\|H'(\Dsep)\|\) from the derivative of the two-center \(\Omega\).
T3a replaces the crude sup-norm route by weighted Hellmann--Feynman matrix elements in the low-mode sector.
T3c performs the literal variable-box pullback \(z=L(\Dsep)y\), with \(L(\Dsep)=\Dsep/2+6\), so that changes of the axial box, kinetic scaling, measure, and boundary terms are tested on the same reference coordinate.
The current T1--T3c package supports this statement on the audited finite grid, with the strongest numerical evidence coming from the literal variable-box coordinate pullback.
This package remains optional; it does not change the baseline map.
In the current certificate, T3a gives \(\max_n\|P'_n\|\le \PRDShortTThreeAMaxProjectorDerivative\), while T3c gives adjacent rank-one projector drift at most \(\PRDShortTThreeCRankOneDrift\), first-three cluster drift at most \(\PRDShortTThreeCClusterDrift\), and pullback-versus-G1 low-three eigenvalue drift at most \(\PRDShortTThreeCLambdaDrift\).

The conceptual payoff is limited but useful.
It allows the labels \(N=1,2,3\) to be discussed as coherent low-mode projectors along the scanned \(\Dsep\) interval, provided the certified gaps remain open.
It does not convert the occupancy closure into a chiral family-count theorem, and it does not retune the release map.

\section{Higgs-Diagnostic Map}
\label{sec:higgs}

The \(H\to\mu\mu\) comparison is retained only as a reference-normalized scan diagnostic.
Schematically,
\begin{equation}
    \mu_{\mu\mu}^{\rm pred}(\Dsep,\eta)
    =\left|\frac{C_{eH}^{\mu\mu}(\Dsep,\eta)}{C_{eH}^{\mu\mu}(\Dsep^0,\eta^0)}\right|^2
    \left[\frac{\Gamma_{\rm tot}(\Dsep,\eta)}{\Gamma_{\rm tot}(\Dsep^0,\eta^0)}\right]^{-1}.
    \label{eq:hmumu_pred}
\end{equation}
The reference point \((\Dsep^0,\eta^0)\) is a normalization convention, not an independent derivation of the absolute muon Yukawa coupling.
We therefore describe this comparison as a reference-normalized diagnostic target region rather than experimental validation.

The flavor-smearing object used in this diagnostic is a nearest-neighbor row-stochastic kernel,
\begin{equation}
    K_{\rm fl}(\epsilon_{\rm mix})
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
    1-\epsilon_{\rm mix} & \epsilon_{\rm mix} & 0\\
    \epsilon_{\rm mix}/2 & 1-\epsilon_{\rm mix} & \epsilon_{\rm mix}/2\\
    0 & \epsilon_{\rm mix} & 1-\epsilon_{\rm mix}
    \end{pmatrix}.
    \label{eq:flavor_kernel}
\end{equation}
It is not a projector:
\begin{equation}
    K_{\rm fl}^2\ne K_{\rm fl}
\end{equation}
for generic \(\epsilon_{\rm mix}\).
Its eigenvalues are
\begin{equation}
    1,\qquad 1-\epsilon_{\rm mix},\qquad 1-2\epsilon_{\rm mix},
\end{equation}
so the release positivity guard keeps \(\epsilon_{\rm mix}\le1/2\).
This wording is deliberately more prosaic than ``projector'' because the matrix is a bounded mixing kernel, not an idempotent projection onto a physical subspace.

The observed ATLAS input is used only through the one-dimensional target value \(\mu_{\mu\mu}=1.4\pm0.4\)~\cite{ATLAS_Hmumu}.
The short paper therefore reports the fraction of scan points in a simple \(\chi^2\le4\) target band and the best grid value, but it does not claim a look-elsewhere-corrected exclusion, a global posterior, or an absolute Standard Model EFT Wilson coefficient measurement.
For the fixed reference-normalization point used in the release figures, the target-band fraction is \(f(\chi^2_{\mu\mu}\le4)=\PRDShortHmumuChiTwoLeFour\).
The long companion's reference-normalization audit varies the reference point over 53 anchors and gives
\begin{equation}
    f(\chi^2_{\mu\mu}\le4)\in[0.0333,\,0.2411].
    \label{eq:hmumu_ref_sensitivity}
\end{equation}
This spread is not a failure of the diagnostic; it is the reason the short manuscript treats Fig.~\ref{fig:hmumu_diag} as a target-region visualization rather than as experimental support for a unique absolute normalization.
Turning Fig.~\ref{fig:hmumu_diag} into a genuine prediction would require an independent normalization theorem for \(C_{eH}^{\mu\mu}\), a nuisance-parameter model, and a pre-specified likelihood.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The present closure has four front-facing limitations.
First, the projected two-center background is fixed rather than derived from a complete EYMH parent.
Second, the spectral label \(N\) is used as an EFT generation-layer label, while a protected chiral-family theorem remains a conditional companion route.
Third, the no-fourth statement is finite-domain and operator-chain specific.
Fourth, the Higgs comparison is reference-normalized and cannot be read as an absolute prediction without an independent normalization theorem.

These limitations are not cosmetic; they define the honest boundary of the current release.
They are also what makes the short paper safer for PRD review: the core claim is computational and conditional, while stronger first-principles completions are separated into the supplement or future work.

\subsection{What would close the limitations}

The first limitation would be closed by deriving Eq.~\eqref{eq:projected_poisson}, including the positive regulated two-source density and \(C^2\) control of \(\Omega\), from a parent variational or constraint problem.
The \(C^2\) requirement is not optional because \(V_{\rm eff}\) depends on both \(\Omega\) and \(\nabla^2\Omega\).

The second limitation would be closed by a bridge theorem identifying the current transported low-mode projectors with protected chiral fermion zero modes.
The optional \(\mathrm{Spin}^c\) package gives a conditional template, but it requires geometric and no-exotics assumptions that are not consequences of the present scalar spectral closure.

The third limitation would be closed by replacing the finite-volume no-fourth certificate with a continuum-tail and \(D_{\rm sep}\)-continuous theorem for the same operator class.
That is a strengthening of the current certificate, not a reason to reopen high-\(N\) visibility damping or introduce a new family.

The fourth limitation would be closed by an absolute matching theorem connecting the release \(C_{eH}^{\mu\mu}\) kernel to a standard SMEFT normalization without using the same \(H\to\mu\mu\) measurement as the reference point.
Until such a theorem exists, the Higgs map remains a diagnostic.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The PRD-short version of PSLT should be a calibrated computational-physics manuscript: it defines a projected two-center spectral problem, a rank-2 occupancy closure, and a release-grid diagnostic showing first-three-layer concentration under stated assumptions.
The long paper remains valuable, but its role is technical certification rather than front-door exposition.
This split gives reviewers a clear target: evaluate the conditional EFT closure and its reproducibility, without being asked to accept every optional mechanism branch as part of the main claim.

The most important scientific output of the short version is therefore not a slogan about ``explaining three generations.''
It is a reproducible map from a projected two-center operator to a normalized spectral-layer occupancy distribution, together with explicit gates showing where the current closure is robust and where it is only diagnostic.
If future work promotes A1 or A2 to parent-side theorems, the same short-paper architecture can absorb that strengthening.
Until then, the defensible PRD claim is the conditional one stated in Eq.~\eqref{eq:claim_schema}.
